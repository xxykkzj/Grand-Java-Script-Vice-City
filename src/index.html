    Show & tell · three.js in the browser

    From an info page to an experience

    Today: a typical government info page

    • Long blocks of text and a PDF brochure to download
    • An opening-hours table and a small photo carousel
    • Visitors read about the place but don't get a feel for it
    • The same page on every screen, little reason to stay

    With three.js: the state museum, in the browser

    • Walk the galleries before you visit: plan a school trip, check accessible routes and lifts
    • Turn objects in your hand: 3D scans of artefacts you can zoom, rotate and light
    • Hotspots tell the story: tap an object for its caption, audio guide and sign-language video
    • Exhibits that can't travel: fragile or archived pieces shown to regional schools
    Why it fits the web No install or app store. It runs on phones and laptops, and a whole world can ship in about a megabyte, like this game.
    Do it responsibly Keep the real text in HTML for screen readers and search. Respect reduced motion. Offer a plain 2D fallback, and set a performance budget.
    More uses Heritage sites, planning and zoning maps, station wayfinding, and emergency drill walkthroughs.
